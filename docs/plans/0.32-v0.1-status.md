## Known faults and limits

- The fence is verified only on this Mac's TeX Live 2024. It is off on Linux.
- Loom never runs a project's `.latexmkrc`, so an Overleaf project that depends on one typesets without it.
- Overleaf names a commit for its last editor, so who made a change cannot always be told; loom lists what it cannot attribute and says when it left changes out.
- A result that opens with a formula is placed in its PDF by position, and marked so.
- Review's two main sections are interim.
- An agent in a quilt can act with the person's authority; the pilots are to be told.
- The lane on this Mac is not what GitHub's CI runs (Linux, Python 3.11 to 3.13, other fonts, a disk that tells case apart). On 2026-11-24 that difference hid a red `library-web` for a day and a red `main` for an hour.

## Picking it up without the cost

The way this ran on 2026-11-24 (seven lanes, five long-lived reviewers, every report through one coordinating session) found real faults and cost too much. For what is left:

- Two pieces at a time.
- A fresh session per piece, started from that lane's "where it stands" note; the old long-lived sessions are not resumed.
- An independent look only for pieces that touch a person's files, tokens or compiles: one look, a fresh reviewer, a narrow bar. Otherwise the diff is read.
- Small jobs done directly and merged in batches.
- Whole test lanes once per piece and at each merge; after every push, GitHub's CI is read for that head, and a red job is a failed lane.
- Suggested order: the node store's part C; the author's look and what it turns up; Install's and Overleaf's viewer halves; "Settle as it stands"; then Fast, Markdown, the sweep and the introduction; Lean last unless he says it holds the release.
